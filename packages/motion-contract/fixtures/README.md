# Fixtures

All three fixtures are generated by `scripts/generate-fixtures.mjs` (a one-off script — the
committed JSON is the actual fixture; re-run and hand-edit if a scenario needs to
change) and validated against `schema/motion-result.schema.json` by
`npm run validate` / `pytest`.

## `good-lesson.json`

The "everything worked" case: 14.0s at 15fps (210 samples), one dancer, a plausible
30-count dance loop. Every joint on every sample is `observed`. Demonstrates:

- A full turn away from camera and back, from t≈5.6s to t≈8.4s (`root_trajectory`
  yaw), tracked continuously the whole way through — the honesty-boundary case-1
  showcase (DESIGN.md §7h): this is the product's best claim, and nothing here
  drops to `uncertain` or `absent` while the dancer's back is turned.
- `grounding.status: "grounded"` with a real floor plane.
- `accent_color.source: "sampled"`.
- Hands and feet crop rectangles present on every sample.
- `proposed_counts`: a **strong** beat proposal — steady 120 BPM, confidence
  0.93, count-1 confidence 0.97, no warnings. The case where the machine guess is worth opening the
  authoring surface on.

## `failure-lesson.json`

The "this will happen often" case: a shorter 6.0s clip (90 samples) exercising
three real-world failure modes at once:

1. **Feet cropped for most of the clip → no floor.** Feet joints
   (`left_ankle`/`right_ankle`/`left_foot`/`right_foot`) are `visibility: "absent"`
   with `provenance.suppressed: "out_of_frame"` from sample 8 onward, and
   `crop_rects.feet` is `null` for the same range. `grounding.status` is `"none"`
   and `floor_plane` is `null` — per DESIGN.md §10, never fake a plane.
2. **Mid-playback dropout + re-entry.** The left arm
   (`left_shoulder`/`left_elbow`/`left_wrist`/`left_hand`) is occluded by
   something crossing in front of the dancer from sample 30 to 50 (t = 2.0s–3.33s)
   and returns to fully `observed` at sample 51 — a viewer seeking across this gap
   should see the contract recover cleanly on the other side, driven entirely by
   `sample_times_s` (never by `array_index / fps_nominal`, since that arithmetic
   is exactly what breaks across a dropout).
3. **`observed` AND `interpolated` AND `suppressed` at once.** Within that same
   dropout window, most samples for the left-arm chain have
   `provenance: {observed: true, interpolated: true, suppressed: "low_confidence"}`
   — the estimator still produced a low-confidence value that also got
   Kalman-smoothed, and it stays flagged `low_confidence` because confidence never
   cleared threshold. A handful of samples inside the window (`i % 4 === 0`) have
   `observed: false` (fully blocked that frame). This is the literal case the PRD
   calls out: provenance is not a single mutually-exclusive enum.

`accent_color.source` is `"fallback"` here, illustrating the other branch of that
field (sampling can fail on a harder clip — OPEN-DECISIONS.md E4).

4. **A weak beat proposal that says so.** `proposed_counts` is 196 BPM at
   confidence 0.28 (count-1 confidence 0.12: which beat is 1 is a guess too),
   with a warning naming the likely cause. 196 is outside the
   70–180 dance-practice band, which is the signature of a double-time lock; the
   correct reading is the `half-time` alternate, which travels with the guess
   instead of being thrown away. This is the branch that matters for DESIGN.md
   §7h — a surface that renders this identically to `good-lesson`'s 0.93 is
   presenting a bad guess as a fact.

`two-dancer-lesson.json` deliberately has **no** `proposed_counts` at all: the
field is optional, absence is a normal outcome (no audio track, silent clip, the
beat stage never ran), and a consumer must handle it without special-casing.

**Seeking** is not a separate fixture — it's a property of consuming either
fixture correctly. Any viewer that seeks by looking up the nearest index in
`sample_times_s` (not by dividing by `fps_nominal`) will land on the right frame
in both fixtures, including inside and across the dropout above.

## `two-dancer-lesson.json`

The multi-dancer case (W8, 2026-09-18 scope revision): 8.0s at 15fps (120 samples),
two dancers with **different, unsynced choreography** — the 2026-09-18 scope
revision made this the normal case, not a violation (`evaluation/clips.yaml`'s old
`violator-duet` entry, which refused exactly this, was repurposed into
`violator-crowd` — a dancer-*count* refusal, not a different-roles one). Each
`persons[i]` carries its **own** `animation` ref (`person_1` → `track_id: 7`,
`person_2` → `track_id: 12`, matching real ByteTrack ids and the one-GLB-per-
confidently-tracked-dancer shape `services/motion-api/modal_app.py`'s
`export_clip_gltf` actually produces) — this fixture is the concrete case that
motivated moving `animation` from a single top-level field into `PersonResult`
(previously there was no way to say which GLB/clip belonged to which dancer at
all). The two dancers' paths cross around t≈3.3–4.3s; during the crossing,
`person_2` (passing behind `person_1`) goes `visibility: "uncertain"` with
`provenance.suppressed: "low_confidence"` — matching `tools/process_clip.py`'s
documented non-goal that no cross-dancer identity-continuity correction happens
in the pipeline, so a real crossing produces exactly this kind of low-confidence
span, not a clean handoff.

**Not a real pipeline run.** No GPU session was available this pass to actually
run two dancers through the pipeline end to end, so this is hand-built to match
the real pipeline's output shape exactly (same field shapes `run_clip` /
`export_clip_gltf` produce) rather than derived from the single-dancer fixtures.
`model_report.measured_performance` here is a plausible placeholder (~2x the
solo-01 gate cost, per the scope-revision's own linear-cost estimate), explicitly
NOT a measurement — a real two-dancer run must replace it before trusting those
numbers for anything.
