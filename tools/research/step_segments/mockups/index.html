Learn it in steps: UI mockups

Research only, 2026-09-24. Round 2 of docs/research/step-by-step-learning.md. It uses real step data from lesson bhangra (job_5716…): chunks 1–3 at all three detail levels, the lead-in and the outro, from ../out/steps.json. The lesson page's own CSS and tokens are copied in. The stages are placeholders (a drawn figure), not playback.

It is one responsive file. View it at 1440×900 (desktop), 390×844 (phone upright) and 844×390 (phone on its side). The dashed "Mockup state" menu jumps to any of the 20 states. It is review chrome, not part of the design. Everything else is clickable. Space plays. On desktop, N goes to the next step and Shift N goes back.

Principles (owner note, applied)

  1. Progressive disclosure. The page shows the current step and one action. Rename, split, merge, the ½-count nudge, skip, the detail level and "Include this" all live behind one Steps button (a panel on desktop, the sheet on a phone).
  2. One primary action per state: "Got it · …". There is no second button. Watch again is the Play button, because the loop is exactly this step. Put it together waits after 4 loops and says "Press play to go again".
  3. The dancer is the hero. Nothing is drawn over the stages except the count band, which is already there. The guided card is the smallest dock that works.
  4. No more controls than free practice. While guided, the count band is a readout rather than 8 loop buttons, because the guide sets the loop. With the empty timeline readout and the single action, guided A shows 6 fewer controls than free practice at every size (table below).
  5. Generous spacing and big targets. On a phone the one action is 88px tall. Every other control in the card is at least 44px, and the existing transport keeps its sizes.
  6. Tokens from lesson.css. The mockup uses the same tokens (paper, paper-sunk, line, stage, ink, one accent), Bricolage and Switzer, and the same pills, chips and panels.
  7. Hide rather than shrink. At 844×390 the path is reduced to dots, the suggestion's second sentence and the exit label are hidden, and the clock goes. Type sizes stay the same.

Three layouts, scored on density

A. A step card docked under the stages (picked)

One slim dock sits between the count band and the transport. It holds the chunk, a dot path, the counts, the cue and one button. On a phone upright it sits above the transport. On a phone on its side it fills the empty part of the right-hand rail.

B. The step list is the guided UI

A rail on desktop, or half the screen on a phone, with the list always open. Every step is visible, which suits editing. It is the busiest option: +11 controls and +21 lines over A on desktop. The stages lose 400px of width on desktop and 130px of height on a phone.

C. Steps on the band, with a floating card

It has the same controls as A and the stages stay full size. But the card sits on the dancer. It covers 12% of the stages on desktop, 31–36% on a phone on its side and 34–48% on a phone upright, usually over the feet and legs.

Density: visible controls / text lines, step card state

The counting script is window.density() in guided.html. It counts visible button, link, input, select, role=button and role=slider elements, plus distinct text line boxes. The live row ran the same script on stepwise.sreshta-talluri.workers.dev (lesson job_345b…, default state, no loop). The live page has about 9–11 more controls than the mockup's copy of free practice, mostly the timeline's section-start buttons and the credit link. Why A

  • The calmest option that still makes the flow obvious. It has the fewest controls, tied with C, and never covers the dancer. It shows one button that says what happens next ("Got it · put 1–2 together").
  • It fits the no-scroll rule at every size. A JS check across all 20 states at all three sizes found scrollHeight ≤ innerHeight, scrollWidth ≤ innerWidth, no dock content clipped, and the phone's primary button 88px or taller.
  • B and C still add something. B's list is the Steps panel, one tap away. C's nesting is on the count band (a step bar under the counts) and the timeline (a chunk band and step ticks).

Data: what is real, what is assumed

  • Real (steps.json, bhangra): step times and counts at all three levels, with chunks 1–3 having 4/4/5 steps at Beginner (the default), 5/5/6 at Intermediate and 10/12/14 at Advanced. Also real: end_cue (chunk 3, step 5 is 0.41, so its cut is shown as a guess), dance_span and idle (a lead-in to 0:04.5 because the dancer is "not in frame, still, walking", and an outro from 0:46.8), and fast_quarters (counts 41 and 43, so Advanced says there are no quarter-count hits in these chunks).
  • Written for the mockup: the cue text. It is taken from each step's moves in the v1 style (8 words at most, the dancer's side). Beginner cues were tidied by hand. The auto text at the other levels is rougher.
  • Invented to show a state: an unsure left arm (chunk 2, step 2) and a cue the grounding check dropped (chunk 2, step 4). These chunks have neither.
